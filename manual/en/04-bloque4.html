The records map and the map studio

4.1The map as a quality check

No automatic rule sees what a trained eye sees in thirty seconds of looking at a map. Before extracting a single environmental variable, it pays to look at the points and ask yourself five questions.

  1. Are there points in the sea? A coordinate in the ocean a few kilometres off the coast is almost always a poorly georeferenced coastal record; far from the coast, it is a sign error or a transcription error.
  2. Does the distribution have the shape you expect? The pinyon pine should trace the two Sierras Madre. If a uniform cloud appears over the high plateau, something is wrong.
  3. Are there suspicious alignments? Points lined up along a road are roadside records: legitimate, but they show where people collect, not where the species lives.
  4. Is there a lone point very far away? A botanic garden, a cultivated specimen or, now and then, the most interesting find of the whole study.
  5. Are the subtaxa where they should be? Colouring by taxon reveals at a glance a subspecies in the wrong place.

4.2What the map shows

The map plots the records that survived Block 3, coloured by the criterion you choose: by taxon, by record type or in a single colour. On top of it you can switch on the cartographic elements (legend, scale bar, north arrow, grid) and the outline of the region you chose in Block 2.

iThe base map comes from the internet

The background tiles are downloaded from an online service. Without a connection, the map shows the points over a plain background, which is still perfectly usable, and the scale bar and the coordinates keep working. If a base map does not respond, the app switches to another one by itself.

4.3The map studio

Below the map sits the map studio, folded away until you need it. Its seven tabs cover everything that separates a map on screen from a publishable figure.

TabWhat it controls
Base mapThe background: greys, streets, topographic, terrain, imagery or none; its opacity and how far it is toned down in greyscale, brightness, contrast and saturation. The Muted look button makes the background discreet with a single click.
Text and fontsTitle, subtitle and credit, and the typeface of each element separately: the title, the legend labels, the scale bar numbers, the grid coordinates and the north arrow letter, each with its own size, bold, italic and colour.
ElementsWhat is drawn: legend (inside or outside the map, in one or several columns), scale bar, north arrow, coordinate grid and frame.
PointsShape, size, outline and transparency of the symbols, and the heat map for when the points pile up.
ColoursThe palette for the taxa or the groups, and the colour ramps for the continuous maps of Block 9.
LooksReady-made presets, and the option of saving your own look as a file to reuse it in another figure or another study.

4.4Exporting for publication

The Export tab answers the three questions any journal asks: what is shown, at what size and in what format.

ControlOptions and what they are for
FramingAs it looks on screen, Fit to the data, Fit to the chosen states or regions, The whole of Mexico and Manual extent with its four coordinates. The padding and the zoom fine-tune refine the result without touching the map on screen.
SizeAs shown, A4 landscape or portrait, single or double journal column (85 and 180 mm), a 16:9 slide, square, or a custom size typed in millimetres.
ResolutionA multiplier for the on-screen size, or 150, 300 and 600 dpi when the size is in millimetres. Most journals ask for 300 dpi for halftone figures and 600 for line art.
FormatPNG (lossless, supports a transparent background), JPEG, WebP, TIFF, georeferenced GeoTIFF and SVG (vector, with the data and the text, but without the base map).
θWhich format to choose for what

PNG for a document or a presentation. TIFF when the journal requires it or when you are going to keep editing the map in a design program. GeoTIFF if you want to open the image in a mapping program and have it land in its place on the globe: it carries the coordinates and the reference system inside. SVG when you want to change the text or the colours later without losing sharpness; since it does not include the base map, it is mainly useful for figures of points on a plain background.

4.5The preview and the clip

Two features spare you the back-and-forth of exporting, looking at the file, correcting and exporting again.

The clip to the study area leaves out of the image everything that is not the country or the regions chosen in Block 2: the background tiles, the suitability surfaces and the points outside disappear, leaving only the silhouette of the territory, with its outline drawn and the outside transparent or in the colour you choose. It is the way to deliver a map of Mexico (or of a single state) without the neighbouring country peeking in.

1:16M
Key fact
The preview works out the approximate scale of the figure from its width in millimetres and the extent it covers. It is the number a map’s figure caption asks for, and the one almost nobody remembers to calculate.

4.6What a publishable map needs

ElementWhy
Scale barWithout it no distance can be judged. A bar is better than a “1:16,000,000” text: the bar remains valid if the figure is reduced.
North arrowEssential when the map is not oriented with north at the top, and good practice always.
CoordinatesThe grid with its degrees lets readers locate any point and compare with other maps.
LegendWhat each colour and each symbol means, with the scientific names in italics.
Base map creditTile providers require attribution; the app writes it in the corner by itself and it is exported with the image. The figure caption should also give the source of the records, how many there are and their date.

4.7Common problems

What you seeWhat happened and what to do
The map shows the whole worldIt was drawn before its space had a size. Press Zoom to the data, in the corner of the studio.
The background is grey, has no detail or carries a watermarkThere is no connection, the provider did not respond, or it adds its mark under its terms of use. Switch base map (the greys are the most reliable) or use None (background colour): for a figure of points, a plain background is usually enough.
The image does not match the screenThe framing is not As it looks on screen. Open the preview, or use Take the map on screen to this framing.
The lettering comes out tiny in the figureWhen exporting to a paper size, the text is sized with the figure. Increase the font size of each element in Text and fonts and check the preview again.
The points pile up and nothing can be made outReduce the symbol size, increase its transparency, or switch to a heat map in the Points tab.
