2.1Filtering means deciding the study area

A filter looks like a matter of convenience —removing whatever gets in the way— but in niche modelling it is a substantive decision. The records you keep define which environments count as “occupied”, and the territory they span defines where the “available” environment they are compared against comes from. Changing the study area changes the model, even when the algorithms and the variables stay the same.

θThe accessible area

In the introduction we saw the BAM diagram: M is the part of the world the species has been able to reach during its history. A model should learn by comparing the occupied sites against that M, not against a whole continent. If the area is too large, the model faces a trivial task —telling the Mexican sierras apart from the Amazon rainforest— and its quality measures come out inflated. If it is too small, the model never sees the conditions the species avoids and learns nothing. A reasonable M is usually the biogeographic region where the species lives, plus a margin.

2.2The four facets

The first card shows four groups of checkboxes with the number of records in each category. Changes take effect at once: every click recalculates the number on the result card.

FacetWhat it is really for
Taxonomic rankSeparating the species from its subspecies and varieties. If you are going to model the whole species, keep them all; if your question is about one infraspecific taxon, keep only that one —and check that it has enough records (section 1.7). It is also useful for comparisons: model each subspecies separately and see whether they occupy different environments.
CountryThe quickest way to remove specimens cultivated on other continents and to confine the study to one country. If you already knew which countries you would work with, it is better to filter them in the download itself (section 1.4): here you filter what has arrived, there you avoid bringing it in. Beware: if your species crosses a border —as the Mexican pinyon does between Mexico and the United States— removing the neighbouring country truncates the real niche, not just the map.
Record typeLIVING_SPECIMEN are living plants in collections: botanic gardens and arboreta. They almost always have to go. PRESERVED_SPECIMEN are herbarium specimens, whose identification can be checked but whose georeferencing is sometimes coarse; HUMAN_OBSERVATION are observations, with a precise coordinate but variable identification.
YearDiscarding old records whose site may have changed its land use, or keeping a period that matches the climate of your layers. If you use WorldClim 2.1, the climate represents 1970–2000: mixing it with records from 1900 is comparing different things.

2.3Filtering by state or region

Many studies are regional rather than national: a mountain range, a state, a watershed. The program comes with the 32 states of Mexico and their boundaries built in, and it can assign each record the state it belongs to.

Where each record’s state comes from

GBIF supplies a text field with the state, but many records arrive without it and others carry it misspelt. That is why there are three modes:

ModeWhat it does
from the coordinates when the text is missing
(recommended)		Uses the record’s text when there is one, normalising spellings and abbreviations (“Edo. de México”, “Mexico”, “Michoacan” → the same state), and when there is none it decides from the coordinate. It is the mode that makes use of the most records.
always from the coordinatesIgnores the text and relies only on the point-in-polygon test. Useful when you suspect the text field is unreliable.
from the record text onlyKeeps whatever the publisher wrote. It is for auditing the data, not for modelling.
▸The example, state by state

With the 2,400 records of the Mexican pinyon: 1,697 got their state from the record text, 22 got it from their coordinates —Mexican records that GBIF delivered with no state— and 681 remained unassigned, which are exactly the 670 from the United States and the 11 from Europe and New Zealand: there is not a single Mexican record without a state.

The states with the most records are Coahuila (246), Durango (208), Baja California Sur (200), Chihuahua (156), Nuevo León (142) and Zacatecas (130): the Sierra Madre Occidental, the Sierra Madre Oriental and the Sierra de La Laguna, which is where the Mexican pinyon lives.

!When the text and the coordinate disagree

In the example there are 54 records whose written state is not the state their coordinate falls in. There are three causes: misspelt names or municipalities taken for states, records right on the boundary between two states —where an error of 200 m changes the answer— and plainly wrong coordinates. The app warns you and does not decide for you: in the recommended mode the text wins, in the coordinate mode the coordinate wins, and badly georeferenced records are dealt with in Block 3.

Shortcuts and options

  • The group buttons —North-west, North-east, West and Bajío, Centre, South Pacific, Gulf, Yucatán Peninsula— tick all the states of that region at once. They work as a toggle: if the group was already complete, a second click removes it.
  • all · none · only with records are the list shortcuts. The last one leaves ticked only the states that have at least one record, which is a convenient starting point.
  • Use the selection as the rectangle turns the chosen states into the rectangle of the geographic clip in section 2.4, and that same bounding box is later offered as the map extent in Block 9.
  • Draw the outline on the maps draws the boundary of your selection on the maps of Blocks 4 and 9, so that it is clear which region is being discussed.
  • Load my layer (GeoJSON) replaces the states with your own polygons: municipalities, watersheds, protected areas, management units, ejidos. The app takes the name of each polygon from its properties and builds the list just as it does with the states.
iWhere the boundaries come from

The 32 states come from Natural Earth at a scale of 1:10 million, which is in the public domain, simplified to keep them light. They are accurate enough to classify records and to draw outlines on a page-sized map, but they are not an official boundary: do not use them to measure areas or to settle a boundary dispute.

2.4Geographic clip

When the region you are interested in does not match any state —an elevation belt, a rectangular study zone, the area of a project— there is the clip by coordinates.

The bounding box and the state filter can be used together: the box clips and the state selection refines. If you are going to model, the box should be somewhat larger than the known distribution, so that the model also sees conditions the species does not occupy.

2.6How to choose the study area

There is no single rule, but there is a sequence of questions that works:

  1. What is the question? A national study of a species calls for the whole country; an assessment of a management unit calls for that unit and its surroundings; a comparison between subspecies calls for the area of both.
  2. How far has the species been able to spread? That is the biological criterion: the biogeographic region it occupies, not the rectangle that fits on the screen. Barriers —a desert, a sea, a mountain range— make good limits.
  3. Is the contrast represented? The area must contain sites with conditions similar to those the species occupies and also sites that are clearly different. An area that contains only its habitat prevents the model from learning what it avoids.
  4. Can I justify it in one sentence? If the answer is “because it looked good”, go back to point 2.

2.7Common problems

What you seeWhat happened and what to do
The count does not drop when you tick a boxThe facet counts refer to the downloaded set. If you had already filtered by another facet, the number on the result card does drop, but the one next to the box does not change.
Many records with “no state assigned”Normal if your download includes other countries: no Mexican state applies to them. If they are Mexican, check the assignment mode: with from the record text only, every record GBIF delivered without that field is left out.
A state with records you did not expectIt may be a little-known real population, a cultivated specimen or a wrong coordinate. Look at it on the Block 4 map before deciding.
The bounding box leaves out records you wantedRemember that longitude is negative in the Americas: a box from −104 to −97 does not include −110. The Use the selection as the rectangle button saves you from typing the numbers by hand.
Your GeoJSON loads but the list is emptyThe polygons carry no recognisable name in their properties, or the file is in a projection other than latitude–longitude. Save it in geographic coordinates (EPSG:4326).
Fewer than fifteen records remainThe area is too small for modelling. Enlarge it, or lower the goal to describing the environment of the points (Blocks 5 and 7) without fitting a model.
